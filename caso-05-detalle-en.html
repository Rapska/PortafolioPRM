Digitization & standardization · Case 05

Request ticketing system

From loose chat requests with no record or follow-up, to a form with ticket number, priority, and a documented response.

DHL Global Forwarding
Role

Design and build of the form and the log

Tools

Google Forms · Google Sheets · Email automation

Status

In use, active

The problem

Given my profile, the team often turns to me for help or to request things get built — anything from help signing up for the company's health insurance to requests to build AI-powered document comparison tools. Those requests came in loose, over chat or spoken out loud, with no record and no way to follow up on them.

What I did and why

I put together, informally and almost as an internal experiment, a ticket-opening form with three fields: email (recognized automatically), priority (high, medium, or low), and the request as free text. Every request gets logged in a spreadsheet with ticket number, user, date, priority, and status, plus a response field that I fill in myself. When a ticket is created, the user gets an automatic email with their ticket number so they can follow up; when it's closed, they get another email with the response I logged.

The result

The team now has a formal, logged channel for asking for help, without depending on me remembering or finding the message in chat. Requests range from simple paperwork to full automation projects — a sign that the team sees me as a point of resolution well beyond trade-compliance tasks.

What I learned

Not every tool comes from a big operational problem. This one started from something as simple as not losing track of informal requests, and ended up becoming the channel the team uses to ask me for anything, from a simple errand to a full project.
