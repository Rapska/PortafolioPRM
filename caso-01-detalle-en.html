Process optimization & continuous improvement · Case 01

OC↔PO lookup automation

From a week to a day to issue the Shipping Order, with an operational pilot live in under a week while the implementation team was estimating eight months for a full solution.

DHL Global Forwarding
Role

Proposal, design and build of the pilot

Tools

Google Sheets · Apps Script

Status

In production, team standard

The problem

An international supplier needed to know the Purchase Order (OC) linked to their Production Order (PO) before they could issue the Shipping Order (SO). This request came in every week from up to three different suppliers, each sending between one and three requests, and the lists of POs to check could range from a handful to over a hundred. Everything was handled by email, and the full cycle could take almost the entire week: the supplier would ask on Monday, the coordinator would check the information manually and reply that same Tuesday, the supplier would see the reply on Wednesday and issue the SO just in time for it to show up in the system on Thursday and get logged for tracking on Friday — the same day that, per the carrier's requirement, the allocation request (space on the vessel) had to be submitted for the following week. That left only a single day of buffer, and any hiccup along the way complicated the whole process.

What I did and why

The bottleneck wasn't the information itself, it was waiting for a manual reply. I proposed a self-service lookup tool (Google Sheets + Google Apps Script) so the supplier could resolve the OC↔PO relationship on their own, without waiting on an email: they can paste a full list of POs copied straight from Excel, with no limit on quantity, and check the status of all of them at once. The implementation team had estimated around eight months for a complete solution; rather than wait for that build, I asked for authorization to build a narrow-scope pilot — it was live in under a week.

The result

The pilot moved into production and is now the team's standard lookup platform, updated weekly. Suppliers went from issuing the SO on Wednesday (or often Thursday) to issuing it on Monday (Tuesday at the latest) — three full days of buffer against the carrier's weekly allocation deadline. Exceptions (POs with no linked OC, usually samples or marketing goods) get resolved as soon as they're spotted.

The original lookup log is still kept alive too: I use it today for quick manual searches, and every lookup on a PO with no linked OC gets logged with date and time, which helps prioritize which ones to create first.

What I learned

The right solution isn't always the most complete one. A narrow pilot, live in under a week, solved the real problem before an eight-month build ever came to exist — and it ended up becoming the foundation of what the team uses today.
