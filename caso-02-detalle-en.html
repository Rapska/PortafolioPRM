Process optimization & continuous improvement · Case 02

Document compliance KPI

A client complaint with no data to back it — until an objective indicator showed only 6.5% of the real delays were the team's responsibility.

DHL Global Forwarding
Role

Interim team coordination; design and build of the spreadsheet and the KPI

Tools

Google Sheets

Status

Active, in daily use

The problem

For document tracking, the Customs Agency would email the team an Excel report listing the shipments that, at that moment, showed as "missing documents" on their platform. Two problems kept repeating: first, by the time the team received the report, several shipments were already updated — the information arrived stale. Second, all five coordinators received the same email, each one downloaded the file, updated the information for their own shipments, and replied with their own "updated" version. The most frequent problem: two or more coordinators would send their own version without including each other's comments, creating confusion about which document was actually the most complete.

What I did and why

While I was building a better tool, I proposed an immediate agreement among the five coordinators: coordinate in a cascading order — if the coordinator before you in the sequence hadn't sent their update yet, the next one would wait before sending theirs. It resolved the confusion right away, with no need for any new tool.

To replace the emailed Excel, I created a global spreadsheet in Google Sheets, keeping a format similar to what they already knew but unifying the "status" criteria across coordinators. I added a traffic light that measures, based on each shipment's ETD (estimated time of departure), how much time is left before the document submission deadline: green up to 7 days after departure, yellow between day 7 and day 12, red between day 12 and the 15-day hard deadline. Once the documents are submitted, the light turns blue, and after review, the row gets archived into the historical log. If documents are submitted past the deadline, the row gets flagged "LATE" and the coordinator has to check who was responsible for the delay.

The result

I built the spreadsheet with a KPI in mind: for a long time, the client's perception — and complaints — was that "everything gets submitted late." The KPI measures the percentage of late documents against the total documents submitted, with a standard that started at 15% and has since dropped to 10%, plus a root-cause log for every delay.

The first measurement, in January 2026, showed that 12.7% of documents were late — but only 6.5% of those cases were the coordinators' responsibility (the rest fell mainly on the shipper: around 10% of the time they didn't send the documents on time, and 45% they sent them on time but with errors). The data made it possible to show the client, with numbers, that their perception was wrong: the team was meeting the deadline in the vast majority of cases.

What I learned

Before you have a better tool, sometimes it's enough to sort out how the team coordinates: the rotation agreement resolved the confusion right away, while I built the spreadsheet that would end up replacing it entirely.
