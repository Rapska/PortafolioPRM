Other cases · Hapag-Lloyd Chile

Contingency procedure during a system outage

When a failure left the team unable to see confirmed payments, I diagnosed the real cause and designed an alternate way to keep releasing cargo.

Hapag-Lloyd Chile
Role

Customer Service Import Coordinator

Tools

Coordination with Collections

Period

2019 – 2022

The problem

The connection between the payment portal and Hapag-Lloyd's system went down, and the team was left unable to confirm payments received. Every coordinator reacted differently: some told clients to wait until the connection was restored, others routed them to Collections, and others kept them on the line while trying to verify manually. There was no consistent response.

What I did and why

I gathered the team to understand what each person was doing, and found that the problem wasn't missing information — the payment was actually coming through — it just wasn't showing up in our view of the system. I talked to Collections to understand how they could confirm a payment on their end, checked whether we had access to that screen, and proposed leaving a system note confirming the payment received to release the cargo, once I had their approval.

The result

With the procedure approved by Collections, I shared the step-by-step with the team, and we were able to keep responding to client requests and releasing cargo consistently while the connection issue was being resolved.

What I learned

In a contingency, the team's inconsistency is rarely a matter of individual judgment — it's that no one stopped to diagnose the real cause before reacting. Realizing the data actually existed, it just wasn't being displayed, is what opened up a solution instead of just waiting for the system to come back.
